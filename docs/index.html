Step 1 — choose a seasonal model

Each model is shown from its own most recent init: C3S Multi-Model Mean, NOAA CFSv2, CMCC SPS4, DWD GCFS2.2, ECCC canSIPS, ECMWF SEAS5, METFR System 9, NOAA SFSv1 and UKMO GloSeas6-GC5.1 from November 2026; BOM ACCESS-S2 and JMA CPS4 are on the November init with the ENSO plume, and publish no snowfall field. Pick a model and you will be taken to its list of variables — accumulated snowfall over the continental United States, ONI and RONI sea surface temperature plumes for the four ENSO regions, and the 10 hPa zonal-mean zonal wind (the stratospheric polar vortex proxy) against the ERA5 1991–2020 climatology.

NOAA CFSv2

NOAA CFSv2 · 40-member ensemble · init 1 Nov 2026 · read directly from NCEP CPC. The absolute snowfall is ERA5's 1991-2020 climatology plus the CFSv2 hindcast anomaly (an approximation — CPC publishes the anomaly only). The ENSO plume is built from CPC's own published Nino indices, so it carries the member spread. NOAA publishes no 10 hPa winds, so this model has snowfall panels only — no zonal-wind panel.

Snowfall is accumulated snow in inches. Anomalies are computed against each model's own hindcast climate. The 10 hPa charts show every ensemble member, the ensemble mean, the ERA5 daily climatology with 10–90th and 30–70th percentile bands, the 1959–2020 record bounds and the model's own climate mean where one could be built.
